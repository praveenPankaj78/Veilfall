# Chapter Twelve Map: The Ember Oath

## Identity and canonical entry

- **Title:** The Ember Oath
- **Playable hero:** Caelan Vey
- **Canonical entry:** `c12-inner-gate`
- **Location:** Both faces of the Black Gate, visible together through Malrec's widening opening.
- **Opening state:** Caelan, Vexa, the surviving expedition, any partner who crossed, and the Chapter Eleven route allies stand at the inner face. Mortal defenders remain on the Edrath face until the breach permits sight and speech.
- **Active question:** What new promise should replace the failing law of the Black Gate?
- **Chapter answer:** The player writes one of four complete laws: separation, mutual passage, destruction, or mortal stewardship. No hidden score chooses the answer.
- **Next-series bridge:** The chosen law damages the rule that controls dawn. The changed sunrise alters one later route into the Luminous Court without beginning Rook's story.

## Knowledge at entry

### Known

- The old Gate is opening from inside and cannot survive unchanged.
- Malrec believes a merger can restore the daughter he remembers as Elian.
- A voice identifying herself as Elian opposed that method.
- The engine can release stored promises toward Worldroot.
- A total merger would damage the boundaries that make people separate individuals.
- Chapter Eleven established who currently controls the Vathis engine corridor and which allies reached it.

### Suspected

- The recovered Gate Nail fragment can stop Malrec's present opening without restoring the old law.
- Earlier promises can support a new law, but broken or destroyed promises can supply only warnings and scars.
- Malrec is acting through the engine from elsewhere.

### Unknown

- The physical location of Malrec's body.
- Whether the engine voice is the only or objectively correct Elian.
- Whether that voice is living, dead, copied, distant, or contained.
- What law should replace the Gate.
- What the damaged dawn will mean beyond the visible change in its light and paths.

## Malrec and Elian boundary

Malrec's voice reaches the Gate through a Crown oath seal embedded in the engine. His body never appears, and the chapter does not locate it. He argues that separation has already killed people and that merger will restore the daughter he remembers. He actively drives the engine and redirects stored promises.

The voice identifying herself as Elian can expose the engine's false restoration pattern and warn where the release will strike. She cannot choose the new law. The text never confirms her death, singular identity, physical containment, or objective correctness.

## Four Gate laws

### Seal the Cinder Deep

**Wording shown before selection:**

> The Black Gate binds both realms. After its final closing, no person, army, promise, offer, or message may cross. Everyone remains on the side they freely choose before the closing. No ruler may reopen it. The seal lasts until living delegates chosen separately by both realms freely write a replacement law.

- **Bound:** The Gate, both governments, armies, travellers, promises, and offers.
- **Authority:** Neither side operates a passage; separately chosen future delegates may propose replacement.
- **Refusal:** Each person may choose a side before closing, but cannot demand another person's evacuation.
- **Crossing:** None after final closing.
- **Duration:** Until a freely written bilateral replacement.
- **Immediate cost:** Willing allies and relationships are stranded on their chosen side. No consequence-free evacuation exists.
- **Stored promises:** Remain sealed in inert Gate stone and cannot be spent.
- **Caelan's freedom:** He permanently loses the right to cross after choosing his side.
- **World state:** Devils become sealed enemies across a silent border.
- **Changed sunrise:** One band of dawn closes like a barred road; Rook's later sealed approach is unavailable.

### Controlled passage by mutual consent

**Wording shown before selection:**

> A named person may cross only after that person and chosen keepers on both sides record yes. Any signer may withdraw before the crossing. A public joint bench reviews disputes. The passage closes when either realm withdraws its consent. Both realms must renew the law each year.

- **Bound:** Named travellers and the independently chosen keepers of both realms.
- **Authority:** A joint bench with mortal and Cinder Deep delegates; no member owns the Gate.
- **Refusal:** Traveller or either side may withdraw before crossing.
- **Crossing:** Individual, named, witnessed passages only.
- **Duration:** One year per renewal; either realm may close it sooner by public withdrawal.
- **Immediate cost:** Caelan owes the first year of public service. Continuing governance attracts pressure and dispute.
- **Stored promises:** Remain locked and may be released only to their original living owners through public review.
- **Caelan's freedom:** He loses the right to make an unwitnessed crossing during the first term.
- **World state:** Devils become recognised neighbours.
- **Changed sunrise:** A narrow dawn road appears only when two separated witnesses name it; Rook's later approach requires mutual permission.

### Break the Gate

**Wording shown before selection:**

> No Gate and no keeper owns the road between realms. The stone breaks now, and no central authority may close it. Every stored promise returns to its living maker. A promise without a living maker enters Worldroot without a new owner. Crossing remains open until people build another law together.

- **Bound:** The Gate structure and every stored promise; no person is bound to a keeper.
- **Authority:** No central owner. Future limits require new collective law.
- **Refusal:** Individuals may refuse bargains, but cannot close the shared opening alone.
- **Crossing:** Uncontrolled in both directions.
- **Duration:** Permanent unless later people build a new law.
- **Immediate cost:** Released promises strike both faces; uncontrolled armies and offers gain routes. Weak forces suffer greater damage.
- **Stored promises:** All release visibly to makers or Worldroot.
- **Caelan's freedom:** He keeps personal movement but accepts no central power to stop invasion.
- **World state:** Devils become invading powers as well as possible individual allies.
- **Changed sunrise:** Dawn splits into an unstable open road; Rook later gains a dangerous approach.

### Take the Gate into Caelan

**Wording shown before selection:**

> The Black Gate and every promise still held inside it enter Caelan Vey. He may open for one named, willing person and may refuse an army. He may not silence the carried voices, give the Gate away, abandon the boundary, or live wholly in either realm. This lasts until his death or a freely accepted replacement law releases every carried voice.

- **Bound:** Caelan, the Gate, stored promises, and anyone requesting passage.
- **Authority:** Caelan may permit a named willing person and refuse armies; he cannot compel crossing.
- **Refusal:** Every traveller retains refusal. Caelan retains refusal of armies but must hear individual requests.
- **Crossing:** Named individual passages through Caelan's witnessed identity.
- **Duration:** Until death or a replacement law freely releases every voice.
- **Immediate cost:** The Gate and its voices enter Caelan. He permanently loses silence, full residence in either realm, transfer of the burden, and abandonment of the boundary.
- **Stored promises:** Remain distinct voices within him; he cannot spend or erase them.
- **Caelan's freedom:** He becomes the mortal keeper and cannot live wholly on either side.
- **World state:** Devils become voices and neighbours carried within Caelan's boundary.
- **Changed sunrise:** Dawn bends around a human silhouette and answers witnessed identity; Rook's later approach depends on the carried voices recognising him.

## Representative walkthroughs

### Revolt, Futureless, weak resources, sealed Gate

Ansel and two witnesses hold the inner line while named Free Ledger refusers open their own renewal hearing. The workers keep the collar evidence. Caelan stops the engine through the exposure custody rule, hears participant refusals, and uses no Oathfire. The seal strands willing allies on their chosen sides. Caelan returns to the mortal road, chooses friendship, and sees one dawn road close.

### Auction, Pell, neutral return, controlled passage

Pell uses his lock map from behind the shield escort. Price Court dissenters admit only the deed's named force and delay every unregistered force. Caelan completes the public review, regains the related freedom, and stops the opening with the bargained fragment. After choosing mutual passage, every living neutral keeper agrees to joint custody. Caelan rebuilds the fortress ring and separately continues his relationship across the named passage.

### Force, mixed company, hostile Vexa, broken Gate

The exact mixed company holds the damaged engine corridor. Civic damage raises the Compact price, so Caelan honours exactly one witnessed passage. Hostile Vexa supplies no trust shortcut. Caelan breaks the Gate without Oathfire. Stored promises release visibly, the company absorbs the uncontrolled crossing cost, and the future dawn road becomes unstable. Caelan chooses the road and a bounded political truce.

### Crown volunteers, command restriction, gatekeeper

Teren and six volunteers accept a defensive order within their limit. Caelan cannot issue a wider command until he fulfils the restriction. Stopping the opening already fulfils the original door-order ending condition; crossing last is now a voluntary rear guard. He takes the Gate into himself only after reading the permanent loss of silence, full residence, transfer, and abandonment. He remains at the threshold and chooses a relationship conclusion separately.

### Mara or Lysara across the Gate

The crossed partner acts on the inner face. A partner who remained at the fortress speaks only after the opening establishes contact. The shared Oath helps only if active and accepted. After the law, the player chooses partnership, distance, friendship, or closure without changing the military result.

### No romance, destroyed Oaths, admitted theft

Destroyed Oaths provide no power. Living witnesses replace missing authority through slower consent, causing extra damage without blocking completion. Caelan uses the admitted stolen fragment, then submits it to witnessed restitution or retains the public theft claim. He chooses a fulfilled single destination and receives the same world-choice access as a romantic route.
