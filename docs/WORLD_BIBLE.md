# World Bible

## The one sentence truth

The kingdom of Veyra was built on a prison, its crown is the lock, and its royal history is a series of edited failures.

## The world known to ordinary people

Veyra is a wet, iron rich kingdom bordered by the Ash March in the north, the amber farms of Rill in the east, the salt principalities in the south, and a black forest called Gloamwood in the west. Greyhaven is its capital. The city climbs seven hills above a river port and a maze of older foundations.

Most people believe King Aldren IV died during a failed poisoning and recovered by miracle. They do not know the recovered king is one of several possible Aldrens pulled across the Veil. They believe the Hollow Bell is a warning system built during an old siege. It is actually part of the prison beneath the capital.

## The hidden truth

Before the present world settled into one history, many possible worlds existed beside it. The Veil formed when early mages forced one version of events to become real. Everything rejected by that choice became the Elsewhere.

The oldest and hungriest rejected possibility is called the Unwritten King. It is not a god. It is every ruler Veyra might have had and every empire that failed to exist, pressed into one vast mind. It wants a body, a crown, and a history that cannot deny it.

Seven people can touch the Veil without vanishing. Each carries a Witness Scar caused by an event that the world later erased. Maelin is one. The others are introduced through their own series.

## Greyhaven

Greyhaven should feel crowded, wet, vertical, and old enough to hide a second city inside it.

### The seven districts

* Crown Hill holds the palace, Gallows Square, embassies, and the homes of old families.
* Bellmarket is a roofed maze where merchants trade relics, rumors, and counterfeit blessings.
* The Tangle is a leaning forest of tenements joined by bridges and laundry lines.
* Saintless Ward contains hospitals, mortuaries, fighting pits, and shrines to people rather than gods.
* Riverteeth is the port, built between enormous stone flood barriers.
* Glassgarden is the mage quarter. Its windows preserve memories and sometimes play them at night.
* The Low Cells and the buried city lie below all official maps.

### Daily texture

Oil and salt are expensive. Most homes use mushroom tallow. Iron coins leave a smell on the hand. Messages travel through bell codes, painted roof marks, and children paid to run the steep streets. Rain is collected by law because the river turns brackish during summer tides. Noble clothing favors dark wool with bright inner linings. Common meals include onion broth, black bread, smoked eel, hard cheese, and small sour apples.

## Rule of magic

Magic cannot create without subtraction. Every act takes something measurable from the world.

* Glass mages remove a memory and trap its shape in glass.
* Veil walkers remove certainty, causing several outcomes to remain possible for a short time.
* Blood craft removes years from a living body.
* Name craft removes a fact from public memory.
* Divine remnants remove attention. The more power they use, the harder they are to notice or remember.

Magic always leaves a trace. Skilled investigators can read those traces. This keeps magic mysterious without making it a solution to every problem.

## The Quiet Names

The old people of Veyra honored nine fictional powers called the Quiet Names. Their worship ended centuries ago. Modern characters debate whether they were gods, powerful Veil creatures, or stories used to control early kingdoms.

Only three matter in the opening saga:

* Namar kept doors, borders, births, and last breaths. Nym may be its final living fragment.
* Avara kept honest hunger and protected promises made in desire.
* Korven kept graves and judged contracts that outlived either signer.

These powers have no link to an active religion. Their rites, symbols, language, and history are fictional.

## Peoples and transformed beings

Vampires, werewolves, demon born people, and desire eaters are conditions or lineages, not moral categories.

### Vampires

Vampires need living memory carried in blood. Animal blood sustains the body but not the mind. Ethical vampires take small, consensual amounts or drink from the newly dead with prior permission. A vampire who starves loses personal memories first.

### Werewolves

The Gloam curse responds to concealed violence, not the moon. A werewolf begins to change when someone nearby hides an intent to cause harm. Control requires investigation and emotional discipline. Crowded courts are more dangerous than forests.

### Demon born

Demons are descendants of Elsewhere beings that entered during early Veil breaches. Their horns, unusual eyes, and heat are physical traits. Some can hear unfulfilled bargains spoken by the dead.

### Desire eaters

Often insulted as succubi or incubi, these people sense desire as color and taste. They gain strength from freely confessed want and become ill when forced to feed on shame or coercion. Their power makes them natural diplomats, artists, spies, and targets.

## Political powers

### The Crown of Veyra

The crown keeps peace by controlling which version of history becomes official. Its secret archivists remove dangerous facts through name craft. King Aldren opposed the latest erasure and was replaced by a version of himself who would obey.

### The Red Registry

A civil office that records births, deaths, magic debts, and transformed citizens. It appears dull and lawful. In truth, it tracks possible Witnesses for the crown.

### The Lantern Court

An alliance of merchants, thieves, and messenger houses. It sells truth in pieces so no buyer can own the whole story. Rook Sable stole something even the Court refuses to price.

### The Pale Compact

A network of ethical vampires who run blood banks, battlefield hospitals, and quiet escape routes. Their public charity hides a civil war over whether consent can survive extinction.

### The Briar Hunt

Werewolf investigators licensed to pursue hidden killers. The crown uses them as symbols of justice while secretly planting false scents in political cases.

## Technology and travel

The setting resembles late medieval Europe in practical capability without copying a specific country. Steel weapons, crossbows, water mills, printing blocks, surgical tools, sailing cogs, canals, and primitive lenses exist. Gunpowder does not. Magic is rare enough that ordinary engineering still matters.

A fast horse crosses Veyra in nine days. River travel is cheaper and more dangerous. Long distance messages move by relay bells and sealed courier guilds.

## The central timeline

### Four hundred years ago

The first Crown of Nails binds the Unwritten King. The official record calls this the Founding Peace.

### Thirty years ago

Seven infants survive a night that the archives later erase. Each becomes a future Witness.

### Three nights before the game

Maelin sees King Aldren murdered in a room that later has no door. Her memory is cut apart. A second Aldren appears alive. Maelin is convicted in secret.

### The Night of the Hollow Bell

Chapter One begins. The prison floods upward. The buried crown weakens. Maelin and Rook meet in the Hollow Vault.

### The coming year

The capital fractures, neighboring powers invade, transformed citizens choose sides, and pieces of discarded history walk openly. The seven Witnesses may repair the Veil, replace the present world, merge possible worlds, or give one person the power to choose what becomes real.

## Continuity laws

1. Magic always takes something and leaves evidence.
2. No transformed lineage is born evil.
3. Official history can be wrong, but physical consequences remain.
4. A dead person may return only if another possible version existed. The returned person is never identical.
5. Each viewpoint character has information the others cannot safely obtain.
6. Intersections change at least one relationship or future option in both stories.
7. The final threat grows from choices made by people, not from a random ancient evil.
