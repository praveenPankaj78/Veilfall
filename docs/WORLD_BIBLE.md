# World Bible: Edrath and the Outer Realms

## The world in one page

Edrath is a mortal continent held apart from three outer realms by the Concord. Nine World Nails pin reality into a stable shape. They are not ordinary objects. Each is a pact between a place, a people, and one law of nature.

The Nails are failing. Each failure creates a local wonder and a local disaster. The protagonists must travel because each culture holds only part of the Concord's history, and each Nail can be reached through a different kind of courage.

## The origin of the Concord

Before recorded history, worlds touched without warning. A child might walk through a wheat field and return as an old woman. Devils could claim a spoken promise. Celestial judges could remove a city for violating a law it had never heard.

Orivane, last of the star dragons, chose to end the crossings. She gave her heart willingly. Mortal smiths divided it into nine living embers. Elven root singers, goblin engineers, orc wind callers, human oathkeepers, and coastal witches placed the embers around Edrath. Together they created the Concord.

The act saved millions. It also cut away lives and places that could not fit one stable history. Those possibilities fell into the Unsea.

## The nine World Nails

Each Nail binds one law. Damaging one causes a specific kind of failure.

1. Greyhaven Nail: distance. Broken roads fold, repeat, or lead to the wrong shore.
2. Shard Coast Nail: memory. Dreams and lived events begin to exchange places.
3. Thornweald Nail: growth. Forests remember extinct forms and reclaim settled land.
4. Brassreach Nail: making. Tools invent purposes their makers never intended.
5. Dragonspine Nail: fire. Heat gains hunger and cold begins to burn.
6. Ember Steppe Nail: death. Ancestors return inside storms, but not always whole.
7. Serekh Nail: truth. Mirrors show possible motives and lies become physical shadows.
8. Black Gate Nail: promise. Spoken bargains acquire force, even when misunderstood.
9. Worldroot Nail: identity. It binds every person to one continuous self.

The first eight form a circle around the ninth. Worldroot cannot be reached safely until enough outer Nails have been healed, moved, or deliberately broken.

## Mortal regions

### Greyhaven and the Crownlands

Greyhaven is a rain dark western port built where the King's Road meets the sea. Its river walls, crowded courts, ship markets, and old fortress introduce the political world without trapping the saga there.

Humans are the majority, but goblin merchants, elven envoys, orc caravan guards, and witches are ordinary sights. The Crown claims authority over the World Nails while publicly denying they exist.

Story purpose: Caelan's stable life, the first betrayal, and Rook's criminal network.

### Shard Coast

Hundreds of black islands rise from green water. Storm bells hang from cliff villages. Witches navigate by tasting rain and store memories in blue glass. The islands elect a Tide Mother for seven years, then forbid her from ruling again.

The culture values witnessed truth because magic can alter memory. Private memories are treated as property.

Story purpose: Ilyra's home, the moral rules of memory magic, and the first proof that history was cut apart.

### Thornweald

This northern forest grows in rings around Worldroot's hidden roots. Elven cities are coaxed from living trees and move slowly over decades. The elves are long lived, not immortal. They record decisions in cultivated groves that can be questioned by later generations.

One faction wants to seal Edrath forever. Another believes the Concord has made the world spiritually sterile.

Story purpose: patient politics, living history, temptation through belonging, and the path to Worldroot.

### Brassreach

Goblin city states fill volcanic calderas north of Dragonspine. Suspended bridges connect foundries, markets, bathhouses, and elected counting halls. Goblins are famed for compact machines powered by heat, pressure, and rune logic.

They distrust inherited rule. An invention belongs partly to the maker and partly to everyone endangered by it. The failing Nail is making machines develop strange preferences.

Story purpose: inventive heists, social comedy with stakes, class prejudice challenged through action, and the creation of tools that can cross realms.

### Dragonspine

A chain of mountains divides the continent. Dragons once nested in the high calderas. Most now sleep beneath ice or live disguised among shorter lived peoples. Their graveyard is a valley of glass formed when dragonfire met eternal snow.

The last open sky road requires either a dragon's consent or a dangerous climb through the hollow bones of one.

Story purpose: awe, vertical danger, the truth of Orivane's sacrifice, and Caelan's transformation from soldier to mythic protector.

### Ember Steppe

Red grass rolls beneath constant wind. Orc clans travel in vast wheel towns built around herds and ancestor shrines. Leadership is earned for a season and can be challenged through service, debate, craft, or combat.

The orcs do not worship death. They treat the dead as honoured witnesses who must never rule the living. The failing Nail causes ancestral storms to speak with stolen voices.

Story purpose: large scale pursuit, mobile warfare, communal duty, and a direct test of whether old promises should control living people.

### Serekh

Serekh is a desert kingdom built along a river that vanishes underground at noon. Its capital surrounds a mirror lake. Witches, scholars, traders, and exiles meet beneath mirrored awnings that make concealed emotion visible as colour.

The royal line rules by passing an ordeal in which every possible motive appears before the court. The truth Nail's failure lets skilful liars weaponise those reflections.

Story purpose: intrigue in bright open spaces, seduction and deception, an illusionist's worst environment, and the route into the Unsea.

### The Black Gate

The southeast is a field of black stone where nothing grows. A gate carved from one piece of star iron opens into the Cinder Deep. Eight human and orc fortresses surround it, each responsible for one watch of the year.

The gate cannot be destroyed without releasing every promise it has ever contained.

Story purpose: Caelan's finale and the first world scale change.

## The outer realms

### Cinder Deep

The Cinder Deep is not a place of punishment. It is a realm where desire, promise, and price become physical. Devils build cities from contracts, trade years of sensation, and wear forms shaped by what observers expect to desire or fear.

Devils cannot break the exact words of a sworn bargain. They excel at making the words incomplete. Some want Edrath opened for conquest. Others fear that a full merger will destroy the rules that let them remain themselves.

### Luminous Court

The Luminous Court is a radiant realm of terraces, white rivers, bells, and living laws. Its celestials believe suffering comes from uncontrolled choice. They are beautiful, sincere, and terrifying when certainty becomes cruelty.

The Court maintains the Name of Dawn, the law that makes each new day continue the history before it. Rook's final theft targets this law.

### Unsea

The Unsea is a dark ocean filled with stars beneath the surface. Islands form from discarded possibilities: lives never lived, cities never founded, children never born. Visitors can meet a convincing lost person, but cannot easily know whether that person is a surviving soul, a possible self, or a hunger wearing memory.

The Unsea offers Malrec real evidence and false certainty. It is the source of the saga's deepest temptation.

### Worldroot

Worldroot exists beneath Edrath and touches all realms. It is not heaven, hell, or an afterlife. It is the living structure that lets one event lead to another and one person remain themselves through change.

The final decision happens here only in Ilyra's series.

## Peoples and narrative function

- Humans build large institutions quickly and often mistake reach for wisdom.
- Elves preserve long memory but risk treating tradition as consent from the dead.
- Goblins solve practical problems through shared invention and fierce public argument.
- Orcs organise around chosen duty, seasonal authority, and mobile communities.
- Dragons embody memory at a geological scale. Their power makes change difficult.
- Witches practise local, negotiated magic rather than forming one species or nation.
- Devils turn desire and obligation into law.
- Celestials turn law into identity and struggle to understand mercy.

No people is uniformly noble, savage, wise, corrupt, comic, or seductive. Culture creates pressures, not fixed personalities.

## Political powers

### Crown of Asterra

The western human kingdom controls Greyhaven and claims the King's Road. Regent Malrec Vale rules while the young queen is ill. His public aim is to prevent panic and foreign invasion. His hidden aim is to reunite the worlds.

### Root Assembly

Elven groves send speakers whose authority lasts only while their home trees remain in agreement. The Assembly can act slowly, but its decisions survive generations.

### Brass Compact

Seven goblin cities share designs, defence, and liability. They will help repair the Concord only if all peoples gain control over the resulting technology.

### Red Moot

Orc clans meet wherever three wheel towns cross. They have no permanent capital, making them difficult to conquer and difficult to unite.

### Tide Council

The witches and captains of the Shard Coast protect the right to own one's memories. They consider the Crown's magical archives a form of theft.

## Magic principles

1. Magic changes a relationship between things. It never creates a result without a source.
2. Power has a visible cost before the player commits.
3. Repeated power use creates story consequences, not just an empty bar.
4. Mortal magic is flexible but limited. Outer realm magic is stronger but bound by rigid law.
5. A person cannot be made to feel love through magic. Desire can be imitated, attention can be influenced, and memory can be altered, but these acts are violations that close romance paths.

## The antagonist

Malrec Vale was a royal astronomer before becoming regent. During an early attempt to repair the Greyhaven Nail, he briefly remembered a daughter named Elian whom nobody else had known. He found her letters in the Unsea and heard her answer him.

He now believes the stable world is built on murder. He removes the Nails to restore every discarded life. He conceals the likely cost from allies and from himself.

Malrec should remain understandable even when he becomes unforgivable. The player may expose him, kill him, bind him, join part of his plan, or complete a safer version of his aim.

## Revelation ladder

The saga reveals its central truth in this order:

1. A royal escort route changes after it is sealed.
2. Roads and records disagree.
3. A World Nail exists and has been damaged.
4. Other cultures guard other Nails.
5. The outer realms are pressing against Edrath.
6. The Concord was a sacrifice, not a natural law.
7. The Unsea contains discarded lives.
8. Malrec is trying to recover one specific person.
9. Restoring everyone without a new law will destroy individual identity.
10. The protagonists can redesign the boundary rather than merely save or break it.

Each answer should create one clean new question.
