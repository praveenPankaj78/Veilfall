# Chapter Six Map: The City on Wheels

## Player promise

The chapter answers one main question: will the clans of Kharad Vey help carry Caelan's ember toward the Black Gate?

The answer is conditional. They help only if Caelan treats their right to choose as part of the future he is protecting. A Crown title, an old treaty, and fear of the Gate are not enough.

The amount of support is also earned. Honest disclosure about Vaor's ember and Korran's personal respect allow the player to request war. Mixed trust allows a guarded alliance or a safe road. If Caelan stole the ember and refuses to admit it, the Moot offers only the road.

The next hook is concrete: a Crown army is one day behind Kharad Vey, and its soldiers are obeying commands spoken by dead officers inside an ancestor storm.

## Continuity entering the chapter

- Caelan always carries Vaor's ember.
- If Vaor gave the ember, the dragon is a free and independent ally who may refuse a request.
- If Caelan took the ember, it obeys him while Vaor follows as an enemy.
- If they made a pact, Vaor advises Caelan from inside his thoughts and shares grief with him.
- Rook departed through the Underways at the end of Chapter Four. The arrest, bargain, or trust outcome remains as future relationship state, not physical presence on Caelan's route.
- Mara and Lysara remember the romantic, committed, or platonic direction the player chose.
- Ilyra may acknowledge attraction only if neither earlier relationship remains open.
- Surviving proof from Dragonspine affects what Caelan can show, but proof does not replace service among the clans.

These facts remain in state, but the opening recalls only Vaor's ember because it changes what Caelan physically experiences on the steppe. Rook's route and earlier romance choices return only when they affect a present scene.

## Explanation order

1. The opening shows the Ember Steppe, Kharad Vey's twelve moving platforms, and the ancestor storm chasing them.
2. Korran names the Red Moot and the Black Gate only after Caelan has boarded and helped the town.
3. Ilyra demonstrates one visible Threadread connection before explaining what it can and cannot reveal.
4. The Unsea receives a name only after the selected storm test produces evidence.

The journal follows the same order. It cannot confirm a term or conclusion while the player is still on the scene that introduces it.

## Fifteen decision structure

1. Cross the open steppe and reach Kharad Vey's moving western lift.
2. Save the lift handlers while boarding a city that will not stop.
3. Prevent the damaged western axle from destroying the homes above it.
4. Choose one local duty before asking to speak at the Red Moot.
5. Complete the herd, forge, or shrine duty.
6. Answer the ancestor storm when it accuses Caelan of serving the power that erased lost families.
7. Keep the dead voices from steering Kharad Vey into broken ground.
8. Tell Korran what place living clan authority has in the future Caelan wants to protect.
9. Meet Ilyra Fen and decide how to answer her calculated public offer.
10. Help Ilyra trace one ancestor voice without surrendering control of the ember.
11. Decide how much can honestly be promised after discovering the Unsea connection.
12. Choose service, trial combat, or a public Oath as the basis of Caelan's case.
13. Complete the chosen Red Moot test.
14. Break the single command trying to make the dead rule the living.
15. Ask Kharad Vey for war, a guarded alliance, or a freely given road.

Every complete route contains exactly fifteen decisions.

## Adventure routes

### Herd duty

Caelan rides beside red horned cattle while an ancestor voice calls a young rider toward broken ground. The route tests direct rescue, locally learned signals, careful ember use, or accepting lost animals to save every child.

### Forge duty

Caelan enters the lower forge where molten iron is sliding toward the steering ropes. The route tests whether he listens to Dema before using the ember and introduces a failed ancestral casting that endangers the living because nobody wanted to challenge its maker.

### Shrine duty

Caelan protects children being called by dead family members. The scene explains the steppe law in plain language: ancestors may advise, but they may not command. The voice using Caelan's father's private name makes the wider mystery personal without answering it too early.

## Ilyra intersection

Ilyra enters as an investigator with her own objective. She has already read the connections among Korran, the storm, and Caelan's ember. She deliberately makes her first offer public so accepting it changes the room.

Caelan may:

- name the pressure behind her offer;
- acknowledge political need and honest attraction as separate choices, if unattached;
- refuse her pressure while accepting the investigation;
- place the work under Korran's local authority.

Threadread reveals connections, including attraction, but never exact thoughts or consent. Ilyra waits for spoken permission before linking the ember to the storm. Caelan remains the protagonist and owns every final decision.

#### The Unsea discovery

Ilyra names the hidden current beneath erased roads as the Unsea. It is her strongest explanation for the storm, not a fully proved answer. The chapter does not claim that every voice is a true dead person.

The player knows only:

- the storm acquires knowledge after the people it resembles died;
- the selected test proves one additional fact about source, private memory, fear, or active copying;
- some voices may be memories or imitations;
- old accounts describe the Unsea as a place where erased lives leave impressions.

This keeps the mystery curious rather than confusing. Chapter Six proves that the question is real. Later chapters answer which voices are conscious and who is using them.

## Red Moot paths

### Service

Caelan uses the duty the player completed as evidence of how he behaves when local lives and his larger mission compete. He must then state a principle that will still matter after the immediate crisis.

### Trial combat

Caelan faces Varka on two moving platforms. The strongest outcomes come from respecting the local law, learning the moving ground, or saving the champion rather than using the ember as an automatic victory.

### Public Oath

Caelan must bind his own conduct rather than promise obedience from the Queen or the clans. Broader promises produce greater political power and greater future danger.

## End states

### War

This request is available only after Caelan gives an honest public account of the ember and earns Korran's personal respect. All twelve platforms turn toward the Black Gate. Kharad Vey enters the campaign as a nation beside Caelan, not as his army. This provides the largest force and exposes the largest civilian population.

### Guarded alliance

This remains available under mixed trust, but not when a stolen ember is concealed. Korran leads volunteer riders, wind callers, and shield engines under the Red Moot's authority. This gives Caelan a meaningful fighting force while preserving the town's independent road.

### Neutral road

The town refuses war but grants supplies, guides, witnesses, and the right to return. Caelan must protect that freely chosen neutrality when the Crown army catches the town.

Every ending grants equal Wayfire. The difference is military strength, civilian exposure, political authority, and the meaning of consent between allies.
