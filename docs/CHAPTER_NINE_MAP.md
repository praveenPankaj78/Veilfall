# Chapter Nine Map

## Identity

Title: The Price of a Name

Playable hero: Caelan Vey

Canonical entry: `c9-embassy-watch`

Location: Second Fort, declared neutral ground and rebuilt as the first Cinder Deep embassy. A witnessed corridor links it to Fourth Fort. The Black Gate remains the only road into the Cinder Deep.

Immediate objective: recover the missing half of the Black Gate Nail and cross the Gate with allies who chose the risk.

Active question: Can Caelan recover the other half of the Gate Nail?

Chapter answer: yes. Caelan can receive it through a bounded bargain, steal it during the attack, or expose House Sableglass and force its surrender under public law.

Main event: Vexa offers the missing Gate Nail piece for Caelan's true name and one unbreakable promise while mortal and devil assassins attack the negotiation.

Next hook: How can travellers remain free where desires automatically become offers?

## True-name rule

Vexa demonstrates the rule before any dangerous offer. She asks a willing clerk to place a clay token on the table and freely speak the private answer that identifies the self she chooses to be when no role is watching. The token turns toward that speaker and lets Vexa direct one already agreed action with exact precision. The clerk can withdraw permission, and the token goes still.

A true name is not an ordinary full name, birth name, title, signature, or secret word. It is a person's freely disclosed answer to who they choose to be. Voluntary disclosure gives contract magic precise access to that person's promises. It does not transfer the person, create consent, reveal thoughts, compel love, or permit unstated acts. The access lasts only for the accepted bargain unless that bargain clearly states a longer term.

The chapter keeps five states separate:

1. A prepared claim owns nothing.
2. An offer awaits consent.
3. An accepted bargain binds only its stated terms.
4. An owned promise has already been paid for and cannot be widened.
5. A true name freely disclosed gives precision within the accepted terms, not unlimited ownership.

## Revelation ladder

1. The clay token proves that voluntary self-identification creates precision and that withdrawn permission ends an unaccepted test.
2. The Gate Nail piece answers the token, proving Vexa possesses the missing promise fragment.
3. Mortal attackers use Crown lock oil and Malrec's cipher to open the mortal west door.
4. House Sableglass uses a contract seam and collection wire to seize Vexa and the fragment.
5. Route-specific evidence joins the two attacks without merging them: Malrec paid House Sableglass for access, while his mortal agents were ordered to kill witnesses and recover the Nail piece.
6. The payment strip and fragment memory reveal the plan: Malrec will open the Gate from inside the Cinder Deep and release stored promises into Worldroot, breaking the law that keeps each person one continuous self.
7. The missing fragment can be recovered now, but the road beyond turns spoken desires into offers. The next problem is freedom on the Ash Road.

## Defining routes

### Bargain for the fragment

Caelan accepts a complete offer: freely disclose his true name for this agreement only and make one unbreakable promise to return the Gate Nail piece to neutral custody after stopping Malrec's inside opening. The paid price grants immediate precision: the fragment joins Caelan without resisting, closes the assassination seam, and lets the party cross under a named protection. Cutting Vexa's true-name access after alignment requires destroying one exact earlier active Oath and creates an acknowledged breach or release. The return promise remains intact.

Payoff: safest crossing and Vexa or the Compact as an escort.

Cost: the stated return promise persists into Chapter Twelve, and Vexa can identify every use of the Gate fragment made under the bargain.

### Steal it during the attack

Caelan uses the two-sided assault to take the lantern and separate the Nail piece from House Sableglass's collection wire. This is not a free version of the bargain. The fragment resists an unnamed bearer, the crossing is more dangerous, and Vexa's response depends on whether Caelan protected her people and stated the theft openly.

Payoff: no true-name disclosure and no new unbreakable promise.

Cost: the fragment crosses without Compact protection; Vexa becomes an adversarial ally or permanent enemy, and the Ash Road begins at higher danger.

### Expose House Sableglass

Caelan joins route-appropriate mortal evidence, collector evidence, and the attack payment strip before public witnesses. With original or joined records this route is a free expert payoff earned by preparation. Other evidence families require a cost, a living witness, or Vexa's cooperation. Exposure forces House Sableglass to surrender the fragment under its own public collection law.

Payoff: no true-name disclosure, a public Malrec alliance record, and the broadest political coalition.

Cost: the fragment becomes disputed evidence. Caelan must carry it openly, cannot conceal the crossing, and House Sableglass marks the company for collection.

## Oathfire clause cut

Only active Oaths appear. Chapter Eight never resurrects the released Crown Oath or burned Warden whistle promise.

- Red Moot authority: destroys the exact duty that Kharad fighters answer commanders chosen by the Red Moot. Immediate advantage: Caelan can cut Vexa’s true-name access clause after payment. Lasting loss: Korran withdraws formal Moot fighters and records the breach.
- Crown restitution: destroys the exact duty to bring the Concord's hidden victims before the Queen or oppose concealment. Advantage: cuts the clause. Loss: the Crown hearing route loses its Oath-backed standing.
- Clan refusal: destroys the exact duty to defend the clans' right to leave after the Gate is safe. Advantage: cuts the clause. Loss: Korran refuses to cross and the Moot treats future command as suspect.
- Honest command limit: destroys the exact duty to claim only the command the Moot granted. Advantage: cuts the clause. Loss: the full army will not follow Caelan across the Gate.
- Unsea investigation: destroys the exact duty to test which ancestor voices are conscious. Advantage: cuts the clause. Loss: preserved voices lose Caelan as their sworn investigator and Korran carries the duty home.
- Bring the escort home, repair the King's Road, hold Harrowfen, bridge protection, Mara honesty, Vaor grief, or living-command Oaths may appear only if still active and if the person or community relying on the exact promise can be named in the choice.

No destroyed Oath becomes generic fuel. The immediate prose names the broken bond, the dependent person, and the lost option.

## Vexa relationship and intimacy

Vexa receives a persistent relationship record with Trust, Attraction, Respect, Friction, and Intent. Its outcomes support guarded trust, adversarial respect, attraction, refusal, and permanent hostility.

Intimacy is separate from the Gate Nail bargain. It becomes available only after both assassination threats are contained, the fragment price is no longer under negotiation, no person is captive, no magical pressure is active, and Caelan is not committed to Mara or Lysara. Vexa states that either person may stop and leave. True-name knowledge and contract magic are physically set outside the room and cannot affect consent.

One choice accepts the private encounter. The content preference changes only the rendered prose. Fade and detailed text produce the same flag, relationship changes, information, political consequence, and next node. Detailed prose appears only after the player explicitly confirms adulthood at this scene. Both versions reveal that Vexa fears a complete realm merger because Cinder Deep law would turn every passing desire into an involuntary offer and erase the boundaries that let devils remain separate people.

Refusal is a complete path. It can create guarded trust or adversarial respect without romance. Hostility follows an attempted seizure of Vexa, deliberate harm to noncombatant envoys, or forced use of her true-name demonstration.
