# World Map and Travel Logic

## Canonical map

The visual map is stored at `public/art/edrath-world-map.png`.

It shows the mortal continent of Edrath and three planar insets. The insets are symbolic windows, not physical places beyond the eastern ocean.

## Mortal layout

- West: Greyhaven stands at the end of the King's Road.
- Northwest: the Shard Coast forms a stormy island chain.
- North: Thornweald covers the broad green country.
- North centre: Brassreach occupies volcanic calderas above Dragonspine.
- Centre: Dragonspine divides the continent and controls the safest eastward passages.
- East: the Ember Steppe reaches from the mountains to the sea.
- South: Serekh follows the hidden desert river.
- Southeast: the Black Gate stands in dead volcanic land.

## Outer realms

- Cinder Deep connects most reliably through the Black Gate and through willingly sworn contracts.
- Luminous Court connects through dawn, bells, ordered machines, and the stair opened from the Unsea.
- Unsea connects through unstable mirrors, damaged memories, and roads that almost existed.
- Worldroot lies beneath every region and cannot be placed at one surface coordinate.

## Travel scale

Distances are measured in story pressure rather than exact modern units, but journeys must remain consistent.

- Greyhaven to the first Dragonspine pass: twelve normal days.
- Greyhaven to Shard Coast by fast ship: five days in safe weather.
- Dragonspine pass to the nearest Ember Steppe wheel town: six days.
- Brassreach to Serekh through the southern pass: fourteen days.
- Serekh to the Black Gate: nine days by prepared caravan.
- An outer realm crossing may consume minutes for the traveller while weeks pass in Edrath.

Travel can be shortened only by an established route, costly magic, goblin machinery, dragon flight, or a changed World Nail. The story must show the cost.

## Route purposes

### Caelan's route

Greyhaven to King's Road to Mileless Bridge to Dragonspine to Ember Steppe to Black Gate to Cinder Deep.

The route grows from familiar duty into mythic responsibility. Landscapes become wider as Caelan's promises affect more people.

### Rook's route

Greyhaven to Mileless Bridge to Underways to Brassreach to Serekh to Unsea to Luminous Court.

The route moves from enclosed criminal spaces into places where hiding becomes conceptually impossible.

### Ilyra's route

Shard Coast to Thornweald to Ember Steppe to Dragonspine to Serekh to Unsea to Worldroot.

The route follows memory from personal loss through cultural history and finally to the law of identity.

## Map continuity rules

1. A route introduced as blocked stays blocked until a visible action changes it.
2. News travels more slowly than protagonists unless magic, birds, or organised relays are established.
3. Seasons and weather change along long journeys.
4. Injuries, supplies, and companions affect route options.
5. Outer realm time differences are recorded in the save state.
6. Every major return visit shows at least one consequence from the player's earlier decisions.
7. New regions are added only if they perform a function the current map cannot.

## Art direction

The map is an in world parchment atlas made by several cultures. Human road marks, goblin engineering notes, elven leaf motifs, orc wind lines, and witch tide symbols can appear in future detailed versions. No real world religious symbols belong in the map or cosmology.
